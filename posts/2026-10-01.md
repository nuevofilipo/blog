# Teaching legs to walk

*2026-10-01*

## Robot Learning: Fresh Papers

### DeCap: treat smoothness as a *constraint*, not a reward term

"Smoothness as a Constraint for Stable Humanoid Locomotion" (arXiv 2609.24552, ~10 days old). Most RL walking policies get smooth by stacking action-rate and acceleration penalties into the reward. Those terms fight the task reward, and every new terrain means retuning them. DeCap uses **constrained RL** with separate constraint groups for the upper and lower body: the legs stay reactive while the torso and arms are held tightly. On real hardware it cut upper-body action rate by **2.5×** and acceleration by **2.18×** compared with reward-based smoothing, and one fixed set of constraints transferred across terrains.

Why it matters for you: jittery first-gait policies are the classic sim-to-real killer on custom hardware, because they cook motors and excite unmodelled compliance. Before your team burns weeks hand-tuning reward weights, read this together with the CaT approach (constraints as terminations, linked below).

[arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24552) · [HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24552)

### WOLF-VLA: whole-body humanoid locomotion as a substrate for VLA learning

A 2026 framework that treats an optimal whole-body locomotion controller as the layer a vision-language-action policy sits on. This is the same two-layer split your project is heading for: **stage 1 is a robust low-level walking policy, stage 2 is a task policy that commands it**. Use it to think early about the interface between the two: what the walking controller should accept as input (velocity commands? root pose? keypoints?).

[arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.25591)

### Sim-to-real goes beyond robots: a hip exoskeleton policy trained on musculoskeletal models

arXiv 2609.28027 (Sep 23). The authors train assistance timing in simulation against a *human musculoskeletal model* across walking speeds, distill it to use only onboard sensors, and fine-tune it per user with online preference learning. The **teacher→student distillation onto onboard observations** is the same pattern you will use: train the teacher with privileged sim state, then deploy a student that only sees IMU and joint encoders.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28027v1)

## Learn: The Walking Playbook (Part 1)

### A reading path from zero to a walking custom biped

A curated order. Read them in this sequence and you will have the mental model most humanoid labs use today.

1. **Rudin et al., "Learning to Walk in Minutes Using Massively Parallel Deep RL"** (ETH RSL). The origin of legged\_gym and rsl\_rl: thousands of parallel envs, PPO, terrain curriculum. Home-turf knowledge, and the RSL people are on your campus. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11978)
2. **Radosavovic et al., "Real-World Humanoid Locomotion with RL"**. A transformer policy over observation history, trained with massive domain randomization, deployed zero-shot on Digit. It shows why *history = implicit system identification*. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03381)
3. **Lipschitz-Constrained Policies (LCP)**. Smoothness through a gradient penalty instead of hand-tuned reward terms. Read it before DeCap above. [project page](https://lipschitz-constrained-policy.github.io/)
4. **Joint-torque perturbation injection for sim-to-real**. Instead of randomizing a dozen physics parameters, inject learned or random torque perturbations to cover model error. Very relevant when your actuator models are rough. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.06585)
5. **Booster Gym**. An end-to-end open pipeline from training to deployment with zero-shot transfer. A good reference architecture to copy. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15132)

### Concept of the day: asymmetric actor-critic

In sim you know everything: true base velocity, friction, contact states, motor strength. On the real robot you only have the IMU and joint encoders. The trick is to give the **critic** the privileged state, so value estimates are sharper and learning is faster, while the **actor** only sees what the real robot can measure. The critic is thrown away at deployment, so nothing breaks. It is nearly free and is standard in almost every modern humanoid locomotion pipeline.

**Checklist for your custom robot:** before training, write down exactly which signals exist on the hardware, at what rate and latency. That list *is* your actor observation space. Add sensor noise and latency randomization that match it.

## Tools & Repos for the Walking Stack

### mujocolab/mjlab

Isaac Lab's manager-based API running on **MuJoCo Warp** GPU physics. It is lighter than Isaac Lab, needs no Omniverse, and is aimed at locomotion and sim-to-real. If your team hasn't picked a sim yet, benchmark this against Isaac Lab before committing.

[paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22074) · [example: VinRobotics heavy-humanoid pipeline on mjlab](https://github.com/VinRobotics/vinrobotics_mjlab)

### menloresearch/isaac\_asimov

Official RL training framework for the **open-source Asimov humanoid**, built on Isaac Lab, covering training through deployment. It is the closest public analogue to your situation: a custom humanoid with its own training repo. Steal the structure.

[github](https://github.com/menloresearch/isaac_asimov)

### junhengl/mpc-rl

MPC-guided RL for humanoid locomotion and manipulation. If pure RL struggles to find a gait on your hardware, an MPC prior can bootstrap it.

[github](https://github.com/junhengl/mpc-rl)

### unitreerobotics/unitree\_rl\_lab

Isaac Lab implementations of velocity tracking and motion-mimic tasks for G1 and H1. Reading their task configs (rewards, randomization ranges, observation scales) is the fastest way to calibrate sensible defaults for a new biped.

[github](https://github.com/unitreerobotics)

## Outside Your Bubble

### The Alzheimer's gene that attacks the brain's plumbing

New research suggests APOE4, the strongest genetic risk factor for Alzheimer's, may actively damage the brain's blood vessels and sabotage the cellular systems that clear harmful proteins. In experiments, researchers reversed some of these effects. This reframes part of the disease from a "neuron problem" to a *waste-removal and infrastructure problem*.

The systems lesson carries over: when a complex system fails, the visible symptom (plaques, a falling robot) often sits downstream of a boring infrastructure failure (vascular clearance, motor thermal limits, comms latency). Look at the plumbing first.

[sciencedaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/news/top/science/)
