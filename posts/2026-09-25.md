## Machine Learning & AI

### MIT's Tiny Flying Robot Pulls Off 10 Somersaults in 11 Seconds

A new AI control system from MIT lets a miniature flying robot move with insect-like agility, boosting speed by ~450%. The approach combines model-based control with learned residual policies — a strong example of sim-to-real transfer at tiny scale.

[The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/robotics-news/)

### "The Agentic Researcher": Fully Automated ML Discovery

Sakana AI, Oxford, and UBC published a framework where LLMs autonomously generate research ideas, write code, run experiments, and compile manuscripts. A milestone for agentic science — and a preview of what your research workflow might look like soon.

[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15914)

## Outside Your Bubble

### Neuromorphic AI Meets Robotics — Prof. Elisa Donati, ETH

At ETH's Institute of Neuroinformatics, Donati's group is studying how neuromorphic chips — hardware that mimics brain structure — can make robotic interactions faster, more adaptive, and dramatically more energy-efficient. This sits at the intersection of neuroscience, chip design, and robotics. Most ML students never look at the hardware layer. But the next generation of edge robots may not run transformers at all — they might run on spiking neural networks etched into silicon that draws microwatts. If you're at ETH, you can walk to this lab.

[ETH News](https://ethz.ch/en/news-and-events/eth-news/news/2026/06/elisa-donati-when-will-ai-robots-become-part-of-our-everyday-lives.html)
